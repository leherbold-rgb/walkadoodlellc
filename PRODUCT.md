# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Pet owners — primarily dog owners — looking for someone trustworthy to walk their dog. They arrive worried and protective: this is their family member, and handing the leash to a stranger is an act of trust. Their context is everyday life pressure (work, travel, long days) colliding with the guilt of a dog stuck at home. The job to be done: find a dog-walking service they feel genuinely safe handing their dog to, and book it without friction. Success on the site is the moment a wary owner thinks "okay, these are my people."

## Product Purpose

Walkadoodle is a dog-walking service. This site is its marketing/brand front — the first impression and the persuasion surface. It exists to convert a protective pet owner from skeptical to confident enough to sign up or book a walk. Success looks like: the visitor understands what Walkadoodle does within seconds, feels the warmth and reliability of the brand, trusts that their dog is in good hands, and takes the next step (sign up / book / get started).

## Brand Personality

Warm and playful — the happy-dog energy: friendly, joyful, a little whimsical, and unmistakably human. Voice is approachable and reassuring, never stiff or corporate. The personality in three words: **warm, playful, dependable**. The emotional goal is the relief and delight of knowing your dog is loved and looked after — joy backed by trust. Playful is the surface; dependable is the foundation. Both must read at once; warmth without reliability reads as flaky, reliability without warmth reads as cold.

## Anti-references

- **Cold corporate.** No sterile, faceless, enterprise look. No stock-photo handshakes, no navy-and-gray "we are a serious company" energy. This brand has a heartbeat.
- **Generic SaaS template.** No gradient hero + three feature cards + stat counters + "Trusted by" logo wall. The modal startup landing page is the thing to escape, not echo.
- **Designer's caution — the cutesy trap.** "Warm & playful" for a pet brand drifts toward cheap clip-art if unguarded: Comic Sans, cartoon paw prints scattered everywhere, garish pet-store color. Playful must come from real personality, motion, copy voice, and genuine dog imagery — not novelty clip-art. Charm, not kitsch.

## Design Principles

1. **Trust is earned through warmth, not polish.** Pet owners are handing over a family member. Reassurance comes from human faces, real walkers, real dogs, and an honest voice — not enterprise sheen or badge-collecting.
2. **Playful, never cutesy.** Joy and personality without the clip-art reflex. Wit in the copy, delight in the motion, character in the type — held to a high craft bar.
3. **Show the dogs.** This is an image-led brief. Real, decisive photography of happy dogs and the people who walk them carries the emotional weight. Colored panels where a dog photo belongs is a failure.
4. **One clear ask.** A worried owner shouldn't have to hunt. The path from "what is this?" to "I'm in" stays short, calm, and uncluttered.
5. **Distinctive over safe.** Average is invisible. Commit to a memorable brand voice — a specific palette, real personality — rather than the safe, beige, could-be-anyone dog-walking site.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No formal WCAG requirement specified, but the bar stays sensible and usable: body text meets ≥4.5:1 contrast, interactive elements are keyboard-reachable with visible focus, markup is semantic, and all motion ships with a `prefers-reduced-motion` alternative. Real alt text on every meaningful image, written in the brand voice.
